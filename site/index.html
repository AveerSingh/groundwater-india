India groundwater: what is in it, and who has looked

India's groundwater crisis is usually described as a shortage. In much of the country the water is there, it is unsafe, and much of it has never been tested.

  • 110 districts where more than a quarter of monitored wells exceed the nitrate limit of 281 districts with at least 10 wells, 2024 pre-monsoon
  • 94 districts with fewer than three monitored wells in 2024 of 640 districts
  • 116 districts never tested for arsenic districts with no arsenic result in 2023-2024, the years CGWB reported arsenic

What this map cannot tell you

  • It is not a verdict on anyone's water. A district where no monitored well exceeded a limit is not "safe"; it means no monitored well exceeded a limit in that round.
  • Monitoring is uneven. Some districts have dozens of wells, some have one, some have none. The hatch shows where there are too few to say anything as a share.
  • Different wells each year. Few sites are sampled in every year, so the map shows one round, not change over time.
  • Some parameters were tested only in some years. Arsenic and uranium in 2023–24 (uranium also partly in 2021); their limits are shown as values only until checked against the standard's text.
  • Context figures have their own dates. Household water sources are from the 2011 Census; groundwater extraction from CGWB's 2024 assessment; tap connections from the Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard on the date shown.
  • Districts are 2011 Census districts, so wells in districts created since are counted in their 2011 parent.
